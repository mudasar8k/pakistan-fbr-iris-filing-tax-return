# Bank transaction reconciliation

> Not tax advice. For privacy, work from **redacted** statements and never commit originals.

## Why it matters

Bank movements feed both income (profit/interest) and the wealth statement (closing balances). Misreading them is the most common source of an unreconciled wealth figure.

## Rules

1. **Every account, both ends:** record each account's **opening and closing** balance on the correct dates. Do not omit dormant or low-activity accounts.
2. **Own-account transfers are not income or expense.** Moving money between your own accounts nets to zero — exclude it from both sides.
3. **Classify inflows by evidence:** salary, export proceeds, profit, gift, loan, reimbursement, own funds. The credit alone does not prove earnings.
4. **Profit/interest:** capture all profit credits across the **whole year**, and the tax deducted on them — even if IRIS economic-transaction data shows only part of the year.
5. **Bank balance ≠ cash in hand.** Keep them in separate wealth-statement fields.
6. **No double-counting:** if last year combined cash and banks, split them this year without adding to prior-year wealth twice.

## A quick reconciliation sketch

For each account:
```
opening balance
  + credits (classified)
  − debits (classified)
  = closing balance   ← must match the statement
```
If closing does not match, a transaction is misclassified or missing — find it; do not adjust cash or expenses to cover the gap.

## Pass-through example

If PKR 100,000 arrives from a friend and you forward PKR 100,000 the same week for a shared purchase, both legs cancel. It is neither income nor your expense — only a retained fee (if any) would be.
