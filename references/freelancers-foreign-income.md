# Freelancers & foreign income

> Treatment of foreign and export income depends heavily on residency, the nature of services, and current-year law. **These areas commonly need professional verification.** Not tax advice.

## Residency first

Your residency status for the tax year determines the scope of taxable income (e.g. foreign-source income of a resident vs a non-resident) and whether a wealth statement is required. Establish this before classifying income.

## Freelancer / IT & IT-enabled services (export)

- Capture **gross receipts** by client/platform and the nature of services provided.
- Identify the **payment route** (bank, remittance, gateway) and keep statements.
- Some IT/IT-enabled **exports** may qualify for concessional or final-tax treatment and may require registration (e.g. PSEB) or proceeds through proper banking channels. **Do not assume eligibility** — verify current conditions and rates on official FBR sources.
- Record **allowable expenses** only with support; do not estimate to balance wealth.

## Foreign employer / remote employment

- Distinguish **salary** from a foreign employer vs non-salary inward remittances.
- Resident individuals may have foreign-source income taxable in Pakistan, with possible exemptions or **foreign tax credit** — treatment varies by year and by any applicable tax treaty.
- Keep the employment contract, payslips and evidence of foreign tax paid.

## Remittances

Inward remittances are not automatically income. Match each inflow to its underlying reason (salary, export proceeds, gift, own-funds transfer) with evidence before classifying it.

## Flag for a professional

- Final vs normal tax regime selection.
- Export concessions / exemptions and their conditions.
- Double-taxation relief and foreign tax credit.
- Permanent-establishment or business-vs-salary characterization.
